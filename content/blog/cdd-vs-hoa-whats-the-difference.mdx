If you're relocating from Florida, you're probably used to comparing two line items: HOA dues (operations) and a CDD assessment (infrastructure debt). South Carolina doesn't have CDDs — that's a Florida-specific legal structure — so the comparison here works a little differently.

HOA or POA dues fund day-to-day operations of a community: landscaping, amenity upkeep, management. They're set by the association's board and can rise with operating costs.

Some SC communities also carry a separate infrastructure-financing mechanism — an improvement district (like the Nexton Improvement District), a municipal improvement district, or a special tax district. Conceptually, these can work similarly to a Florida CDD (an assessment tied to infrastructure debt rather than day-to-day operations), but they're distinct legal structures under South Carolina law, not CDDs.

Both HOA/POA dues and a district assessment, where one exists, are legally binding obligations tied to the property, not the person — so both transfer to you at closing. Get the exact current-year figures for both before writing an offer.
