A Community Development District (CDD) is a special-purpose local government created under **Florida law** (Florida Statutes, Chapter 190) to finance and build infrastructure for a new community — roads, water/sewer, amenity centers, sometimes even schools. It's a specific legal structure, and it's why people relocating from Florida so often ask about it.

**South Carolina doesn't use the CDD statute.** It's not a generic term for "master-planned community fee" — it's a Florida-specific creature of Florida law. If you're moving here from Florida and see a line item on a listing that looks similar, it's a different legal mechanism, not a CDD.

## What South Carolina uses instead

SC communities can carry several legally distinct fee mechanisms, and a single community can carry more than one at once:

- **HOA or POA dues** — fund day-to-day operations (landscaping, amenity upkeep, management)
- **A regime fee** — similar concept, different label, common in some SC developments
- **An improvement district** (e.g., the Nexton Improvement District) — a special-assessment mechanism that can fund infrastructure, conceptually similar to what a CDD does in Florida, but a distinct legal structure under SC law
- **A municipal improvement district or special tax district** — other SC-specific mechanisms tied to a particular municipality or district

<Callout type="info" title="Don't assume any of these apply without checking">
These mechanisms are not interchangeable, and none of them is a CDD. See our [community cost guides](/neighborhoods) for what actually applies to a specific community — we flag it clearly when it's not yet verified.
</Callout>
