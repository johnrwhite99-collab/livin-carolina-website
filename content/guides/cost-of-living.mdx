Here's the actual state of the numbers a household moving to Charleston should plan for — organized by category, with a clear line between what's a stable structural fact and what varies by property and needs to be checked directly.

## Property tax: the structure is knowable, the dollar amount isn't (yet)

South Carolina assesses owner-occupied primary residences ("legal residence") at a lower rate than second homes, rental properties, or commercial property — this is set by state law and is a stable structural fact, not an estimate. What that translates to in actual dollars depends on the local millage rate, which is set separately by the county, municipality, and school district and varies by location. We don't have current verified millage rates for specific areas yet — confirm the current rate and legal-residence exemption status with the county assessor's office for any specific property before budgeting around it.

## Insurance: homeowners, and possibly a separate flood policy

Standard homeowners insurance does not cover flood damage. Whether a property needs a separate flood policy — and what that policy costs — depends on its FEMA flood zone designation, which can vary meaningfully between nearby addresses. Coastal SC has also seen insurers adjust pricing and availability for wind/hail coverage in recent years. We don't have current verified premium figures to quote here; get an actual quote for a specific property before assuming a number.

## HOA dues and special-district assessments

Many newer master-planned communities in the Charleston area carry HOA or POA dues, and some carry an additional special-district assessment (an improvement district, a CDD, or another mechanism) on top of that — these are legally distinct fees that can both apply to the same property. See our [community cost guides](/neighborhoods) for the specific communities we've looked into, including what's verified and what isn't yet.

## Utilities

Electric service in the Charleston metro is split between a few different providers depending on exact location — confirm which one serves a specific address rather than assuming. Water/sewer is typically municipal or through a regional authority depending on the area.

## Commuting costs

Beyond gas and vehicle maintenance, some of the fastest-growing parts of the metro (the Summerville-area growth corridor in particular) have seen commute times into downtown Charleston lengthen with population growth. If a short commute matters to you, test the actual drive time at rush hour before assuming a map estimate reflects reality.
