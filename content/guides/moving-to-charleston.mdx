Relocating to Charleston involves the same logistics as any long-distance move, plus a handful of things that are specific to this part of the coast. Here's what actually matters.

## Timing the move around hurricane season

Atlantic hurricane season runs June through November, with the highest activity typically in August, September, and October. This doesn't mean you shouldn't move during those months — plenty of people do — but it's worth building flexibility into a closing date or moving-truck reservation during peak season, and worth knowing your evacuation route before you need it, not during a storm watch.

## Flood zones and insurance

This is the single most-overlooked practical detail for coastal SC relocations. Standard homeowners insurance does not cover flood damage — it's a separate policy, and whether it's required (and what it costs) depends on the specific property's FEMA flood zone designation. Flood zones can differ meaningfully between two homes a few blocks apart, so this needs to be checked per-address, not assumed based on general "is Charleston flood-prone" impressions.

<Callout type="warning" title="Check this before you write an offer, not after">
Pull the FEMA flood zone designation for a specific address, and get an actual flood insurance quote, before you're under contract. It can materially change the real monthly cost of owning a specific property.
</Callout>

## Choosing where to land first

If you're relocating without having picked a specific community yet, a short-term rental in the area you're considering — rather than committing to a purchase sight-unseen — is a common and reasonable way to test commute times, storm-season feel, and neighborhood character before buying. See our [Charleston area guide](/charleston-area) for how the different parts of the metro compare.

## The logistics most people underestimate

- **Utility setup**: electric service in the Charleston area is split between a few different providers depending on exact location (municipal utilities, Dominion Energy, and electric cooperatives all serve different parts of the metro) — confirm which one serves a specific address before assuming.
- **School registration**: if applicable, this often requires proof of residency (a signed lease or closing documents), so timing matters if you're moving mid-school-year.
- **Vehicle registration and driver's license**: South Carolina requires new residents to register vehicles and obtain a SC driver's license within a set window after establishing residency — check current DMV requirements, since these rules and timelines are set by the state and can change.
